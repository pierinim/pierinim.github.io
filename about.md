I am a permanent research staff physicist in the Experimental Physics Department at
[CERN](https://home.cern), working on the [CMS experiment](https://cms.cern) at the Large Hadron
Collider since 2007. I was CMS Physics Coordinator from 2022 to 2024, and since 2026 I have been
the AI Coordinator of the CERN research sector (EP, IT and TH departments).

My research focuses on **artificial intelligence for particle physics** and **real-time data
analysis**: using deep learning, and new ways of selecting and recording data, to extend the
sensitivity of LHC experiments to new physics.

### Research highlights

- **Real-time analysis at the LHC**: introduced **data scouting** in CMS, the first demonstration
  of real-time analysis for LHC physics, and the **B-parking** dataset that turned CMS into a
  flavour-physics experiment too.
- **Anomaly detection**: proposed anomaly detection at trigger level as a strategy to search for
  new physics; CMS now runs such algorithms in its Level-1 trigger. Earlier, anomaly detection for
  CMS data quality monitoring, with A. A. Pol.
- **hls4ml**: developed a library to deploy neural networks on FPGAs for the LHC trigger, which
  reshaped the CMS Level-1 upgrade plans and helped secure the funding of CERN's Next Generation
  Triggers project.
- **Graph neural networks**: proposed their adoption in particle physics, on point-cloud
  representations of LHC data.
- **Searches for new physics in CMS**: SUSY (razor, RPV), dijet and diboson resonances,
  model-agnostic dijet searches, and the CMS Exotica Hotline.
- **BSM phenomenology**: the Higgs look-alike matrix-element method, adopted by CMS in 2012 and
  contributing to the Higgs discovery; missing-energy look-alikes, pMSSM, light stops, gauge
  mediation.
- **Global fits**: electroweak and Higgs fits with HEPfit (since 2013); CKM fits with UTfit
  (since 2004).
- **Flavour physics**: charmless B decays on BaBar; charming penguins, penguin pollution in sin2β,
  γ from Dalitz plots and MFV in phenomenology.

I am an author of about 1,900 papers, with more than 133,000 citations (h-index 157).
