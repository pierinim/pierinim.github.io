[hls4ml](https://github.com/fastmachinelearning/hls4ml) is an open-source library that translates trained neural networks into FPGA (and ASIC) firmware via high-level synthesis, achieving microsecond-scale inference latency for trigger applications.

I started and developed hls4ml to deploy neural networks on FPGAs for hardware-based triggers at the LHC; it has grown into the broader *Fast Machine Learning* community. This work started a major effort to bring deep learning into the CMS Level-1 trigger, reshaped the CMS plans for the Level-1 upgrade for the High-Luminosity LHC, and played a key role in securing the donation that funded CERN's Next Generation Triggers project. Highlights include support for binary/ternary networks, convolutional networks (5 µs latency, up to 97% resource reduction with no accuracy loss), graph neural networks and symbolic regression. In 2021–2022 the project received an ERC Proof-of-Concept grant.
