Certifying the quality of the data collected by CMS traditionally relies on shifters looking at
histograms. With Adrian Alan Pol, during his PhD, and with colleagues in CMS (G. Cerminara,
F. Siroky, V. Azzolini and others) and at Université Paris-Saclay (C. Germain), I worked on
automating this with deep learning: autoencoders and other unsupervised methods that flag
anomalous detector behaviour and data, both for online monitoring and offline certification.

Selected papers:

- [Deep learning for inferring cause of data anomalies](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07051), J. Phys. Conf. Ser. 1085 (2018) 042015
- [Detector monitoring with artificial neural networks at the CMS experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.00911), Comput. Softw. Big Sci. 3 (2019) 3
- [Anomaly detection using deep autoencoders for the assessment of the quality of the data acquired by the CMS experiment](https://doi.org/10.1051/epjconf/201921406008), EPJ Web Conf. 214 (2019) 06008
- [Deep learning for certification of the quality of the data acquired by the CMS experiment](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-6596/1525/1/012045), J. Phys. Conf. Ser. 1525 (2020) 012045
- [Anomaly detection with conditional variational autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05531) (2020)
